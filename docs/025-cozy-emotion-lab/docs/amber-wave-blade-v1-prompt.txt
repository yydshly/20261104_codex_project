Use case: photorealistic-natural.
Create ONE original fine-art photograph of water and warm light, square composition, no lettering or border. No input images.

An intimate, powerful ocean photograph made from the water's own level. One oblique swell rises extremely close to the lens, its dark heavy body running from the near lower-left toward the farther upper-right. Wind pulls an off-center section of the crest into a thin, uneven blade of water that is just about to tear. This small unstable section is the visual center: low late sunlight, outside the frame, passes THROUGH it and turns the thin water luminous amber, while the thick body beneath remains deep brown-green. A few long irregular fingers are separating from the edge, then breaking into an uneven scattering of fine droplets blown sideways. The water is still yielding and falling; it must never look like a rigid glass sculpture. No tube, circular barrel or hollow arch.

Build a decisive, asymmetric shape with a palpable change of scale: weighty near water occupying the lower portion, the fragile amber crest a little right of center, the far spray receding to softness. A brief crossing chop presses against the larger swell at its base, giving the rise a natural physical cause. The shape should feel immense from this close water-level viewpoint even though the photograph contains no human or other scale prop. Leave a spare, dark warm backdrop above and beside the crest. Only a faint far horizon is visible through a gap at one edge, with a narrow copper-colored sky, no visible sun.

Warm color is the first priority, tactile photographic detail the second. Honey and copper transmitted light, chestnut highlights, dark olive-brown thickness, deep warm charcoal shadows. A concentrated luminous area rather than gold sprayed everywhere. Let natural surface folds lead toward the bright thin blade. A few trapped bubbles and uneven soft foam beads establish real seawater. The foreground is moving liquid with a small number of stretched bronze reflections, not a field of noisy sparkling points. Keep shadow detail quiet and fluid.

Give the photograph extraordinary dimensional clarity and HDR-like tonal richness: delicate structure survives in the brightest thin water, multiple depths remain visible in the darker body, tiny droplets carry selective bright edges rather than becoming solid gold beads. The breaking edge and its nearest ridges are sharply captured; more distant mist is softer. Natural highlight rolloff, fine photographic grain, clean color and restrained local contrast. No sharpening halos, crunchy texture or murky gray cloud patterns. The water should feel wet, massive, transparent where thin, and alive.

The image should hold the moment between pressure and release. It may feel dreamlike through light, shape and scale, while retaining convincing photographic material and fluid motion. Do not force a resemblance to any animal, wing, face, hand, heart or other emblem. Do not reproduce a known wave photograph's framing. Invent the wave's asymmetry, flow and composition.

No animal, person, boat, building, window, shoes, steps, props, moon, sun disc, stars, rainbows, artificial particles, gold leaf, molten metal, CGI, painted water, theatrical light rays, montage, text, logos, captions or watermark. Only one finished photographic image.
