Use case: photorealistic-natural.
Create ONE original fine-art waterscape photograph, landscape 3:2, without text or border. No reference images.

A mountain lake seen from very close to its surface as a brief heavy rain shower moves away. The image holds two states of the same water at once: near the viewer and to the left, rain roughens the dark surface; farther out and to the right, the lake is almost still and opens into silver-gray light. A softly irregular, oblique edge of retreating rainfall crosses the middle distance, making the change from agitation to quiet visible. This is one continuous place and a believable local weather event, not two photographs spliced together.

The camera is low but not underwater, looking outward from inside the shower. In the immediate foreground, a small group of falling drops has made a few low, transparent water crowns and spreading rings. This one group is clearly resolved and modest in scale, never a giant macro splash. The surrounding rain marks become progressively finer, softer and lower in contrast with distance. Avoid a uniform field of white dots. Broad dark blue-green water remains visible between the rain impacts, giving the eye somewhere to rest.

Across the frame, the rain curtain is heavier at the left and thins as it recedes diagonally into the distance. On the calmer side, a wide smooth silver surface leads toward a very low, distant, blurred line of forest and a barely suggested mountain shoulder. Keep the land small and quiet. The sky is mostly outside the picture or compressed into one broad soft overcast mass; no busy mottled cloud patterns. A subdued opening in the weather gives the calm water natural luminosity without showing the sun or producing theatrical rays.

Color is free of any warm-palette requirement. Use credible cool slate, deep blue-green, pearl silver and charcoal with nuanced neutral transitions, not a blue filter. The atmosphere can feel tense close by and spacious farther away. The physical contrast between nearby falling rain and the unreachable stillness across the lake must carry the emotional quality by itself. No person, boat, isolated symbolic object or posed tree is needed.

Photographic rendering:
Natural environmental water photography, with a tangible sense of wet air, surface tension, distance and subtle shifting light. Transparent low crowns, true rings on the same horizontal plane, a little natural short-streak softness in falling rain. Fine detail belongs primarily to the nearest few impacts; the far lake and forest recede optically and atmospherically. Preserve shadow separation and delicate highlights with restrained tonal control, not aggressive HDR, sharpening halos or equal crispness everywhere. Smooth tonal depth and a very fine photographic grain. Water remains liquid, never glass, polished metal, plastic or smoke. No milky smear across the whole lake.

Compose a clear asymmetric progression from the darker disturbed foreground-left toward the open silver right distance, with an irregular transition rather than a hard diagonal line. Make the image spare and immediately readable while rewarding attention to the nearer water. It should feel photographed during a passing event, not designed as a motivational poster. A coherent natural exposure and physically believable scale.

Entirely independent subject and framing. No circular barrel wave, golden wave blade, marine animal, moon, stars, celestial disc, giant arch, stairs, reflected clouds arranged as a portal, building, window reflection, boots, people, boats, floating props, fantasy particles, lettering, logo, watermark or montage. One finished photograph only.
